Judging Rechoice

Rechoice is a try-on that checks itself. A shopper sending a top back for how it looks sees each eligible replacement on the same person as the top they bought. Where the inputs are available, top previews get three local measurements: colour against the catalog, print against the top bought, and change outside a fixed garment band. A flagged preview holds the quote until the shopper chooses it knowingly.

See the gate in one minute

  1. Open the blouse return with the button below, choose I don't like how it looks and Continue.
  2. Set the limits. Type "The print is too busy. Keep long sleeves. At most $10 extra.", choose Fill in from my words, then Confirm preferences. Stock, policy and budget decide which tops qualify.
  3. Preview on a person. The demo opens on Model B. Tick the photo statement and Request previews: recorded YouCam Clothes V4 results from our registered gate study, replayed at once and spending nothing.
  4. Read the label on each preview. The turtleneck: Checks agree. The sage top came out lighter than its product photo: Flagged: colour, with the product colour beside the colour measured on the preview. Show what changed highlights detected image changes; Can I trust this preview? lists the three measurements.
  5. Choose the sage top. The quote is held until you choose it anyway, with the reason on screen. Then review the quote and create the request; with the code, answer it from the staff queue.

How the gate is tested

  • Study v1, registered before any render: 8 live renders on two people, the first with the blouse on the same photo. All six alternatives measured quieter than the blouse and stable outside the fixed garment band; three were flagged for colour at ΔE 11.1, 13.6 and 19.4. Every measurement.
  • Study v2, built to make the gate fail: 38 live renders on four more people, each labelled by a blind AI rater. Rendered again, every colour flag recurred within 0.2 ΔE. It also found what to fix next: pockets read as print, and colour is not yet corrected for the photo's light. Results and limits.
  • The gate studies' renders, measurements and labels are public: what YouCam actually returned, failures included, and the copilot and AI-review evidence.

Two more one-minute paths

The same exchange flow and same-person comparison work beyond tops:

A lipstick shade that's wrong

  1. Choose The shade looks wrong and Continue.
  2. Type "It looks too orange on me", choose Fill in from my words, then Find shades.
  3. Preview on a person on Face A: the shade bought and two cooler shades side by side.
  4. Opened, so it can only be exchanged within its line, as the store's policy says.
Return the lipstick

Earrings that are too long

  1. Choose I don't like how they look on me and Continue.
  2. Type "These are too long and I wanted silver", choose Fill in from my words, then Find earrings.
  3. Preview on a person on Face A. YouCam drew the crystal drop as long as the pair bought; Rechoice adjusts its relative proportions using the catalog length ratio, with Show it as YouCam drew it to compare. This does not measure physical fit.
Return the earrings

The same order also has foundation, nail polish, a necklace, hair colour, shoes and a bag; It doesn't fit and It arrived damaged take their own routes. Staff see what the store kept in the merchant insights.

Why this problem matters
  • 19.3% of US online sales were expected to come back in 2025 (NRF and Happy Returns).
  • 28% of shoppers who return clothes say it didn't look the way they thought (PowerReviews, 25,452 US shoppers): a problem try-on can show, unlike fit or damage.
  • Yet only 17.1% of US returns become exchanges; 76.1% end in refunds (Loop Returns 2025, 13.8 million returns).

Everything here is fictional: the store, the order and the people, who are AI-generated images. No order, stock or money changes. Recorded previews are real YouCam results from our pilots and studies, so they appear at once and spend nothing.
